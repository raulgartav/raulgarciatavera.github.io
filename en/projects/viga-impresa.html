3D-printed PLA beam

Design, calculation, manufacture and testing of a load-bearing element that clears two obstacles and carries 250 N between supports 500 mm apart. Deflection had to stay at or below 15 mm and the safety factor between 1.5 and 2.5. We solved it with a 3D-printed PLA I-section.

Geometry

The shape of the beam was dictated by two 50 mm obstacles between the supports. To clear them, the beam drops 120 mm between its two horizontal segments, and the 250 N load acts on the lower one. We iterated the angle of the inclined segment and the length of the first segment, and chose 65° and 185 mm. It balances a low bending moment, a reasonable split between axial and shear force, and joints that are easy to make.

  • Support reactions: 96.9 N at A and 153.1 N at B. The maximum bending moment, 29.7 N·m, is at the load point.
  • At 80° the moment in the inclined segment dropped by only 1.9 N·m, but that segment worked almost entirely in compression, with a risk of buckling, and the joints became nearly right angles.
  • Resulting segments: 185 mm upper, 132.4 mm inclined and 259 mm lower.

Material and cross-section

Using Ashby charts we chose PLA (E = 2,800 MPa, 60 MPa strength) printed at 100% infill: it is cheap, accessible and allows a custom geometry. We chose an I-section because it is symmetric and uses both flanges equally in tension and compression. We sized it iteratively with Navier's law, the Rankine criterion and Castigliano's theorem, looking for the lowest mass that met every limit. The result was an 18 × 30 mm section with 2 mm flanges and a 3 mm web. With it, the calculated peak stress is 24.0 MPa (safety factor 2.50), the deflection 13.8 mm and the theoretical mass 107 g.

  • Material: eSUN PLA Basic withstands 64 MPa along the extruded lines but only 28 MPa between layers. That is why the layers had to follow the stress direction.
  • Section limits: height no more than twice the width (so it does not tip over), width up to 35 mm and height up to 38 mm because of the obstacle.
  • We tried every combination of web (2–5 mm), flanges (2–5 mm), width (10–30 mm) and height (16–32 mm). A 22 × 28 mm section was 10 g lighter, but its 14.9 mm deflection sat right at the limit.
  • We also checked the flange-to-web junction (20.9 MPa) and the neutral axis, where shear peaks (1.3 MPa): the critical points are the top and bottom faces.

Manufacturing

The beam is 500 mm long and the printer bed (Bambu Lab A1) is only 256 mm, so we printed it in two parts joined by a lap splice with a steel M4 bolt. Around the joint we thickened the web to 10 mm, which raised the safety factor there to 3.5. We tuned the print settings (230 °C, 0.16 mm layers, 5 perimeters, 100% rectilinear infill) so the properties would match the datasheet.

  • The cut is in the inclined segment, 73 mm from the upper corner. The moment there is 20.9 N·m, and each flange passes about 770 N to the bolt.
  • With the 3 mm web, the bolt crushed the PLA at 56.9 MPa, almost at the 60 MPa limit. With a 10 mm web the bearing stress drops to 17.1 MPa.
  • The section through the hole sits at 16.4 MPa, a safety factor of 3.7.

The first prototype failed

We printed the first beam in PLA Matte, which turned out to have a much lower Young's modulus than the one used in the calculations. We tested it at home and it broke far earlier than expected, splitting between layers in the inclined segment. All layers had been printed horizontally, so in the diagonal segment the normal stress acted partly across them. We reprinted in PLA Basic, orienting each segment so the fibres followed the stress direction.

Finite element check

We checked the hand calculation with SOLIDWORKS Simulation, using a mesh of about 58,000 nodes. The simulation gave an 11.3 mm deflection, against 13.8 mm from Castigliano. The difference makes sense: the 3D model captures the extra stiffness of the corners and the joint, which the analytical model leaves out. Both values are below the 15 mm limit.

  • Solid mesh of quadratic elements: 58,182 nodes and 29,672 elements. The bolted joint was not modelled, because its safety factors were much higher.
  • Peak stresses at the load section: 20.9 MPa in tension and 18.3 MPa in compression, against 24.0 MPa from the hand calculation. The hand calculation is on the safe side.

Automatic colour sorter with a Geneva drive

A machine that sorts parts by colour. A conveyor feeds them onto a platform that turns in exact 90° steps thanks to a Geneva drive. At each stop a sensor reads the colour and three servos push each part into its bin.

The challenge

Coordinating a continuous feed (the conveyor) with a colour reading that needs the part to stand still, followed by physical sorting. The answer was a classic mechanism: a Geneva drive turns the motor's continuous rotation into intermittent 90° steps, and during each pause the platform is mechanically locked.

How it works

  • A conveyor driven by a geared DC motor, with LEGO parts, drops each part at the reading station.
  • A TCS3200 sensor reads the colour inside an opaque housing that shields it from ambient light.
  • A stepper motor (A3967 driver) turns the drive wheel. Each turn rotates the platform 90° and keeps it locked for the remaining 270°.
  • The Arduino tracks the colour of the part at each station and fires the HS-422 servo at the matching one.
  • An LCD shows the detected colour and a count per colour. There is a start/stop button and an emergency stop.

Design and manufacturing

All custom parts were modelled parametrically in SOLIDWORKS following design-for-manufacturing criteria and printed in PLA. A 0.2 mm clearance between the pin and the Geneva slots gives a smooth engagement. Before printing, we checked the assembly with interference detection and a motion study so the servo arms would not hit the sensor mount.

Control

The C++ program synchronises three things: the sensor reading, the platform rotation and the servo actuation. To keep any task from blocking the others, the stepper is timed with millis() instead of fixed delays. The sensor is calibrated with reference parts of each colour before use.

My role

I coordinated the four-person team throughout the project, from the proposal to the final prototype and poster.
